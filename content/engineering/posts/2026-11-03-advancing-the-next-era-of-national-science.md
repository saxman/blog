[Advancing the next era of national science](https://openai.com/index/advancing-the-next-era-of-national-science/)

OpenAI's announcement details its commitments to the U.S. Department of Energy's Genesis Mission, an initiative that unites DOE, its 17 National Laboratories, universities, and industry to integrate AI with federal scientific data, computing, and facilities, aiming to double American research productivity within a decade. OpenAI will provide $4 million in Codex access to about 2,000 Genesis researchers and $3 million in API support for two large-scale campaigns: one pursuing high-temperature superconductors and one building an "Atlas of the Machine-Accessible Frontier" to identify problems AI can already advance. It will also offer up to $10 million in API usage for $2.5 million spent, give selected researchers access to GPT-Rosalind for biology projects, grant trusted lab leaders early access to selected models and features, and expand access to advanced cyber capabilities for defensive research. These commitments build on prior work, including an AI Jam Session in which more than 1,000 scientists from nine National Laboratories tested frontier models, the deployment of reasoning models on Los Alamos's Venado supercomputer, and joint bioscience safety evaluations.

- **Codex access:** $4 million for about 2,000 Genesis researchers.
- **Scientific campaigns:** $3 million in API support for two large-scale campaigns, one pursuing high-temperature superconductors and one building an "Atlas of the Machine-Accessible Frontier" to identify problems AI can newly tackle.
- **API match:** up to $10 million in API usage for $2.5 million spent.
- **Specialized access:** GPT-Rosalind for eligible biology projects, early model access for trusted lab leaders, and expanded cyber capabilities for defensive security research.

These efforts build on prior work, including a 1,000-scientist AI Jam Session across nine National Laboratories, reasoning models deployed on Los Alamos' Venado supercomputer, and joint bioscience safety evaluations. OpenAI stresses that researchers must stay central in defining questions and validating results.
