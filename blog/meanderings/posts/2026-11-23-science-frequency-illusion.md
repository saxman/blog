[Frequency illusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency_illusion)

The frequency illusion, also known as the Baader–Meinhof phenomenon, is a cognitive bias in which a person notices a concept, word, or product more often after recently becoming aware of it. The name "Baader–Meinhof phenomenon" was coined by Terry Mullen in a 1994 newspaper letter, and linguist Arnold Zwicky introduced the term "frequency illusion" in 2005, attributing it mainly to selective attention and confirmation bias. Related effects include the recency illusion and the split-category effect, and theoretical accounts draw on information processing and the natural frequency hypothesis. The illusion has been studied in linguistics, medicine, marketing, economics, and research methodology.
