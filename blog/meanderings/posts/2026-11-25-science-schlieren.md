[Schlieren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlieren)

Schlieren are optical inhomogeneities in transparent media, localized differences in optical path length that deflect light rays, mainly by refraction, producing brightening, darkening, or color changes in an image. First observed by Robert Hooke in 1665, the phenomenon was developed into a practical detection method by August Toepler in 1864, who adapted Léon Foucault's knife-edge test to find flaws in lens glass. Schlieren flow visualization uses a knife edge to partially block undeflected light, turning refractive index (and thus density) gradients into visible shadow patterns. The effect has also been used in projection display technologies such as the Eidophor and Talaria.
